Your home during the project.

Will you repair the drywall and repaint afterward?

Our repiping service includes repairing the wall and ceiling openings made for our work, along with the associated painting. We put the affected areas back together rather than leaving you to arrange the finishing work yourself. Your written proposal identifies the restoration areas and finish approach before work begins.

Will my home look the way it did before?

Our goal is to return the affected areas to their pre-project appearance—not leave unfinished repairs behind. We review existing finishes and explain the restoration approach before starting. If a finish presents a matching challenge, we discuss the solution with you before proceeding.

What happens to the mess?

We keep materials organized, contain project debris, and clean the work areas as the job progresses. Your yard, walkways, and neighboring common areas will not be treated as dumping grounds. Project debris is removed as part of completion.

How quickly will you put the house back together?

We plan the piping work and restoration together so the project keeps moving. Before starting, we explain the anticipated schedule for plumbing work, drywall repairs, painting, and final cleanup. We communicate changes rather than leaving you wondering when the finishing work will happen.

Will I be without water?

The project plan should explain anticipated water interruptions and how they will be coordinated. Discuss your household’s needs with Ted before scheduling.

Your pipes, proposal, and paperwork.

How do I know whether my home has polybutylene?

Start with identification—not the age of your home alone. An assessment considers accessible piping, available records, and previous replacements. Concealed areas may require further investigation.

Can I share photographs or an inspection report first?

Ask Ted how to share your report or clear photographs of accessible piping. These can help start the conversation but do not establish the material or condition of the entire concealed system. Do not cut pipes or open walls to take photographs.

What if some pipes have already been replaced?

Tell Ted what was replaced, where, and when. Share any available invoices or project records. The proposal should distinguish remaining piping from work already completed.

Does repiping include drains?

Not automatically. This page focuses on water-supply piping. Your proposal should identify whether drain work is included, excluded, or separately priced.

What determines the price?

The piping included, home layout, access, replacement material, fixture connections, and restoration scope shape the estimate. The written proposal explains inclusions, exclusions, and how additional work would be authorized.

Who handles permits and association approvals?

Your project plan should identify applicable requirements and who handles each responsibility. For attached residences, clarify approvals and access involving shared areas before work begins.

What documents will I receive?

Confirm the completion records included in your proposal, such as warranty information and any applicable permit or inspection records. Raise insurer, buyer, or association documentation requirements before approving the project.

Will the work satisfy my insurer?

Your insurer decides whether the work and documentation meet its requirements. Share its written request with Ted before the scope is finalized. Coverage, renewal, and acceptance of the completed work remain decisions made by your insurer.
